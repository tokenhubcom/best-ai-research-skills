# Research Reporting

Transform an existing evidence set into a report that lets a reader distinguish observation, interpretation, and recommendation.

## Establish the reporting contract

1. Identify the decision, audience, scope, deadline, required length, and reporting format.
2. Inventory all supplied sources and note obvious gaps, conflicts, date limits, and excluded material.
3. If answering the question requires substantial new retrieval, stop and route that phase to deep research. Perform only minor verification when explicitly authorized and tools are available.

## Build the report

1. Copy [`assets/research-report-template.md`](assets/research-report-template.md) when a reusable file is useful.
2. Build a claim inventory: claim, supporting evidence, counterevidence, confidence basis, and citation.
3. Group evidence into findings by the reader's decision needs rather than source order.
4. Explain why each finding follows from the evidence and where it does not generalize.
5. Draft conclusions separately from recommendations.
6. Make recommendations conditional on assumptions, trade-offs, risks, and ownership. Label an inferred owner as `Proposed owner`; never present a suggested team as having accepted responsibility.

## Evidence rules

- Attach a source URL, file reference, interview label, or user-supplied citation to each material factual claim.
- Label synthesis, inference, and recommendation explicitly.
- Present consequential disagreement instead of averaging it away.
- Do not add unsupported precision, benchmarks, or market claims to make the report sound complete.

## Completion checks

- Answer the decision question in the executive summary.
- Ensure every recommendation is connected to a finding.
- Include scope, method, source coverage, and limitations.
- Separate `not found` from `does not exist`.
- Distinguish confirmed owners from proposed owners and unassigned work.
- Leave a visible placeholder for missing evidence rather than inventing it.
