# Source quality rubric

Score each material source on five dimensions from 0 to 2. Use the score to guide weighting, not as a substitute for judgment.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Directness | Repeats an unattributed claim | Secondary synthesis | Primary evidence or authoritative record |
| Method transparency | Method absent | Partial method | Reproducible method and definitions |
| Authority | Unknown or irrelevant expertise | Plausible expertise | Responsible institution or demonstrated domain expertise |
| Recency and fit | Stale or wrong context | Partially applicable | Current enough and directly applicable |
| Independence | Same evidence chain or conflict hidden | Independence unclear | Independent evidence and conflicts disclosed |

## Interpretation

- **8–10:** Strong candidate for a central claim, subject to topic-specific limitations.
- **5–7:** Supporting evidence; corroborate consequential claims.
- **0–4:** Use only to discover leads, illustrate a viewpoint, or document weak evidence.

## Overrides

- A high score does not repair a retracted, falsified, or irrelevant source.
- Official sources are authoritative about their own policies and data, not automatically about impact or comparative quality.
- Multiple articles derived from the same press release, dataset, or study count as one evidence chain.
- For fast-changing claims, recency may dominate the total score.
- Record why a source was weighted differently from its numeric score.
