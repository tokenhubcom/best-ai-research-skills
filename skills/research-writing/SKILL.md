# Research Writing

Turn existing research into clear content without allowing narrative polish to outrun the evidence.

## Establish the writing contract

1. Confirm audience, purpose, channel, voice, length, desired reader action, and review constraints.
2. Identify the supplied evidence set and whether citations should appear inline, as notes, or in a source list.
3. Refuse to imply firsthand use, customer results, expert endorsement, or quantitative proof not present in the evidence.

## Draft from claims

1. Build a claim ledger before drafting: material claim, source, qualification, and intended section.
2. Choose a structure based on reader questions and decision sequence, not source order.
3. Draft one claim-evidence-meaning unit at a time.
4. Label illustrative examples as examples; do not present them as observed cases.
5. Use quotations sparingly and preserve attribution.
6. Keep unsupported numerical or outcome claims out of publish-ready titles, headings, and calls to action. Use an evidence-neutral working title or an unmistakable bracketed placeholder.
7. Add transitions only after factual coverage and caveats are complete.

## Evidence pass

- Trace material factual claims back to the ledger.
- Remove unsupported superlatives, certainty, causality, and universality.
- Restore dates and boundary conditions that affect interpretation.
- Preserve source disagreement when it changes the reader's conclusion.
- Ask for evidence rather than filling a high-stakes gap with general knowledge.

## Default structure

```markdown
# [Specific title]
## Why this matters
## What the evidence shows
## How to interpret it
## Practical implications
## Limitations
## Sources or further reading
```

## Completion checks

- Match the requested audience and channel without imitating a named living author.
- Keep promotional language subordinate to verifiable evidence.
- Ensure titles and headings do not present unverified outcomes as established facts.
- Do not copy substantial source wording.
- Ensure the conclusion does not introduce a new unsupported claim.
