# Academic Research Writing

Support defensible scholarly writing while preserving academic integrity and the limits of the supplied evidence.

## Establish the academic contract

1. Confirm discipline, research question, document type, institution or venue rules, word count, citation style, source set, and permitted assistance.
2. Follow the user's academic-integrity policy when it is more restrictive than this skill.
3. If the source set is insufficient, identify the literature needed. Do not fabricate references; use deep research when new retrieval is authorized.

## Synthesize before drafting

1. Copy [`assets/literature-matrix.md`](assets/literature-matrix.md) and map each source by question, method, sample or corpus, finding, limitation, and relationship to other sources.
2. Group literature by themes, methods, debates, or chronology as appropriate. Do not produce a sequence of disconnected article summaries.
3. Identify consensus, disagreement, methodological boundaries, evidence gaps, and the proposed contribution.
4. Build an argument outline in which each major claim has evidence and a counterargument or limitation.

## Draft and verify

1. Draft claim-evidence-reasoning paragraphs with cautious language appropriate to study design.
2. Distinguish direct quotation, paraphrase, synthesis, and original analysis.
3. Follow [`references/citation-verification.md`](references/citation-verification.md) before finalizing references.
4. Leave `[citation needed]` or `[source detail to verify]` when bibliographic evidence is unavailable.
5. Check that the abstract, introduction, analysis, and conclusion make the same bounded claim.

## Completion checks

- Do not fabricate citations, quotations, page numbers, data, methods, or results.
- Do not describe a draft as peer reviewed or publication ready without the relevant review.
- Preserve methodological limitations and credible counterarguments.
- Verify every in-text citation against the supplied or retrieved source record.
- Remind the user to perform subject-matter, originality, and citation-style review before submission.
